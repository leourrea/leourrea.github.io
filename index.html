Leonardo Urrea R.

PhD Candidate in Economics, University of Pittsburgh

I study public finance and macroeconomics in developing economies: how governments adjust when debt becomes unsustainable, how fiscal rules perform in practice, and who bears the cost of fiscal and economic crises. Most of my work uses Colombian administrative and fiscal data.

Before the PhD I spent a decade working on fiscal policy from the inside: as a macroeconomic analyst at Colombia's Ministry of Finance, as a researcher and consultant at the Inter-American Development Bank, and as an advisor to the Caribbean Development Bank. I have taught economics at Universidad Nacional de Colombia, Universidad de los Andes, Universidad del Rosario and the University of Pittsburgh.

Research

Job Market Paper

Fiscal Adjustment without Collapse: Restructuring Subnational Debt under Colombia's Law 550

with Gabriel Tourek (UC Davis) · November 2026

Public debt relative to output is at a record outside wartime, and the ways to stabilize it are few: higher taxes, lower spending, inflation, financial repression, or a restructuring. Which one a government chooses decides who bears the cost. We ask whether a government that can no longer pay its debts can restore its fiscal health and pay its creditors without a contractionary or regressive adjustment. Colombia's Law 550 provides the setting: close to a hundred departments and municipalities restructured their debts and adjusted under the Ministry of Finance's supervision without a bailout, beside a thousand governments that never did, under the same monetary policy. We code every agreement and estimate each cohort against the never-treated with synthetic difference-in-differences. The agreements worked. Interest and debt service fell for at least six years, the primary balance barely moved, and the debt stock did not rise faster than in comparable governments. The adjustment fell on payroll and on the government's own capital spending while social investment rose and no tax rate was raised. Output did not contract.

En español

Soy candidato a doctor en Economía de la Universidad de Pittsburgh. Investigo finanzas públicas y macroeconomía en economías en desarrollo: cómo se ajustan los gobiernos cuando su deuda deja de ser sostenible, qué tan bien funcionan las reglas fiscales en la práctica y quién asume los costos de las crisis fiscales y económicas. Buena parte de mi trabajo usa datos fiscales y administrativos de Colombia.

Antes del doctorado trabajé diez años en política fiscal: como analista macroeconómico e investigador del Ministerio de Hacienda y Crédito Público (2012–2018), en el Banco Interamericano de Desarrollo y como asesor del Banco de Desarrollo del Caribe. He sido profesor en la Universidad Nacional de Colombia (2014–2022), la Universidad de los Andes, la Universidad del Rosario, la Universidad de La Salle y la Universidad de Pittsburgh.
